# English content refresh

This change retains the repository's original Jekyll build, content collections, blog archives, URLs, and deployment workflow. The user selected a new visual design inspired by Rhine Lab, implemented with dedicated portfolio layouts and a scoped stylesheet. The final navigation keeps conventional academic labels, and the new CV uses the shared profile/publication data directly.

The biography preserves the original site's undergraduate mentors, robotics laboratory experience, humanoid robot perception/control work, and robotic-arm board-game project. It adds the current UCAS research, Prof. Jungang Xu's supervision, and the Baidu and ByteDance internships. The introduction follows the concise structure common to academic homepages: current position, specific research questions, then earlier education and experience. Wording is original.

Seven publications were reconciled against the original papers. Both IEEE Access papers explicitly name Chengjie Fang as a co-first author, listed second. FG-MoE is third authorship and AnyTemplate is fourth authorship. Two manuscripts are kept under review without assigning an accepted venue, because older records give inconsistent conference/year information. An older AD-PINI manuscript absent from the current CV is not presented as a new active publication.

The original twelve technical posts and seven friend introductions are rewritten in English, preserving useful technical instructions and original routes. Original personal news updates are translated and retained. The old empty project pages now describe related research at their existing URLs; a robotics project page preserves the undergraduate background in more detail.

Academic ranking denominators differ between local records and are omitted. National Scholarship follows the current CV without inventing an award year. No proprietary business performance numbers are invented. Internship cards use official company wordmarks as low-opacity backgrounds, without showing internal-system screenshots or attributing unrelated research figures to either company.
